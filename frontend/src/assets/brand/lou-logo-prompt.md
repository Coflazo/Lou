# Lou Logo Generation Prompt

Use case: logo-brand
Asset type: primary wordmark plus secondary favicon/monogram for a minimal legal workspace named Lou.

Create a restrained, premium legal-operations logo system for "Lou". The brand style is compact, scannable, and sober rather than decorative. Use the existing Lou palette: Ink `#111314`, Paper `#f4f1ea`, Warm Gray `#a1a39a`, and White `#fffaf1`. The logo artwork itself should be white/off-white, designed to sit on an ink background.

Primary logo: a single text wordmark reading exactly `Lou`. Use a humanist sans-serif feel consistent with `Avenir Next` or `Gill Sans`: clean, quiet, slightly warm, professional, and not futuristic. Use normal casing exactly as `Lou`, not all caps. Keep letter spacing subtle, around 0.02em. Avoid slogans, icons inside the word, scales of justice, gavels, shields, gradients, shadows, and decorative flourishes.

Secondary mark / favicon: build a nested monogram from the letters `L`, `o`, and `u`. The letters should be outlined white/off-white strokes, not filled solids, so the nesting remains legible at small sizes. The `L` is the largest letter and forms a crisp 90-degree corner. The `o` sits inside the lower interior angle of the `L`, touching the inner vertical stroke and the inner horizontal foot tangentially, barely kissing both edges. The `u` sits inside the `o`, smaller again, with the bottom of the `u` touching the inside lower curve of the `o` tangentially. All three letters should feel like one typographic construction, aligned and balanced, with no extra container unless making the favicon.

Favicon version: place the nested white monogram on a solid ink `#111314` square with an 8px-equivalent radius. Keep generous padding so the mark remains readable at 16px and 32px.

Output requirements: transparent-background SVG for the wordmark and monogram, plus an ink-background SVG favicon. Keep the geometry vector-clean, flat, one color for the mark, no gradients, no shadows, no textures, no mockup perspective, no 3D, no watermark. The final should feel like a serious legal-workflow product, not a consumer app or law-firm crest.
